# The in-tab node does not run out of main thread at 1080p, and the extrapolation was 2x pessimistic

> ## ⛔⛔⛔ WHAT THE CLIENT UNDER TEST ACTUALLY WAS, AND WHO CHOSE IT
>
> The `weeb3` arms here fetch **segment bytes only** from the in-tab node. The feed and every
> manifest still come from a **bee gateway**, in both conditions. Verified in source: `ManifestManagement`
> has no weeb-3 path, only `CustomManifestLoader` does.
>
> That split was my design decision in PR #183 and **nobody authorised it**. The owner's instruction
> of **2026-08-11T07:07Z** was *"Abel optimized the player as much as possible let's measure and
> experiment with his setup as it is"*, and this is not that.
>
> ⛔ **So any residual gateway load reported below is a floor THIS CLIENT imposes, not one weeb-3
> imposes.** Abel's own live page drives it to zero, proved free on 2026-08-16. Every saving figure
> here is a **lower bound** on what an in-tab node can do.
>
> ✅ **The arithmetic and the arm-to-arm contrasts are unaffected.** Both conditions read the
> manifest the same way, so the comparison is clean. What is limited is the **subject**, not the sums.
>
> See [`abel-gateway-less-live-2026-08-16.md`](abel-gateway-less-live-2026-08-16.md).

Task #105. One live broadcast of 65 minutes at 1920x1080 / 6000 kbps / 0.5s GOP, eight arms of six
minutes alternating `gateway weeb3 gateway weeb3 weeb3 gateway weeb3 gateway`, first round discarded
as warm-up. **2.881 BZZ.** `~/sittings/1080p-2026-08-15`.

Identical in shape to `main-thread-saturation-2026-08-14.md`, so that sitting's 720p arms are the
direct control rather than a number from somewhere else.

## The answer

`main-thread-saturation-2026-08-14.md` closed by extrapolating the in-tab node's peak to **1.38 of
one thread** at 1080p, which is above the ceiling one thread has, and said in as many words that it
was an extrapolation written down so it would get measured. It is measured, and it is wrong by about
a factor of two in the direction that matters.

| | 720p / 2500k | **1080p / 6000k** | growth |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| bytes | 1.00x | **2.40x** | |
| weeb3 main thread, mean | 0.218 - 0.235 | **0.355 - 0.360** | **1.59x** |
| weeb3 main thread, **peak** | 0.545 - 0.641 | **0.559 - 0.707** | **1.18x** |
| gateway main thread, mean | 0.070 - 0.074 | 0.088 - 0.089 | 1.23x |
| gateway main thread, peak | 0.143 - 0.148 | 0.147 - 0.191 | 1.15x |

**The peak reaches 0.707 of one thread against an extrapolated 1.38.** Main-thread cost grows well
below linearly in bitrate: 2.40x the bytes buys 1.59x the mean and **1.18x the peak**.

⭐ **So 1080p is not the wall.** Nothing here says where the wall is, and a straight line through two
points is what produced the wrong answer the first time, so no third point is offered.

## What it cost the network, which moved the other way

| | 720p | **1080p** |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| gateway retrievals per counted arm | 40,242 | **86,079** |
| weeb3 retrievals per counted arm | 1,723 | **1,932** |
| **saving** | 23.4x fewer | **44.5x fewer** |
| gateway BZZ per broadcast hour, gateway arms | 0.60 | **1.69** |
| gateway BZZ per broadcast hour, weeb3 arms | not recorded | **0.01** |

⭐⭐ **The saving nearly doubles at 1080p.** A gateway viewer's retrieval count tracks the bytes and
an in-tab viewer's does not: the gateway arms went up 2.14x with the bitrate while the weeb3 arms
went up 1.12x. The condition ratio on the main thread widens the same way, **3.12x to 4.04x**.

## ⛔ The axis guard refused arm 8, on its first real sitting

`read-sitting.py table` returned non-zero:

    8   4 gateway  counted   83866  1.36  2.03  0.088  0.191  0  3.06  True  25.9  fps 0.864x

    ⛔ REFUSING TO SUMMARISE. 1 counted arm(s) were not delivered at 1920x1080 30fps

**0.864x is the magnitude of the 2026-08-05 failure this guard exists for** (three of four 1080p rows
at ~26.5fps against 30, a ratio of 0.883). It was not that failure. Arm 8 recorded **91 rebuffers
against 0 to 5 in every other arm**, an advance ratio of 0.9498 and 11 stalled samples, so it was a
degraded arm and the frame rate is the shadow of the rebuffering rather than a starved encoder.

⭐ **Refusing it was still right, and the refusal is doing what it was built to do.** The guard's
denominator is the window's wall seconds rather than its media seconds, which is documented as making
a doubtful arm more likely to be refused. Arm 8 was doubtful for a reason the guard does not name,
and excluding it is what a reader should want.

⭐⭐ **The encoder is separately exonerated, for free.** `encoder-capability.mjs` measured this host
at **exactly 30.0 fps, ratio 1.000, at 1080p/6000k**, with a 720p control reading the same, before
any BZZ was spent. So a low in-arm frame rate here is delivery and cannot be encoding.

⚠️ **Arm 8's exclusion leaves the gateway condition at n=2** (0.088, 0.089) against weeb3's n=3
(0.360, 0.357, 0.355). Both still replicate. The two gateway readings agree to 0.001.

## ⛔⛔ WITHDRAWN MID-SITTING: the stalls do not track the byte source

After four arms the gateway arms had 8 and 7 stalled samples and the weeb3 arms had 0 and 0, which
reads as a clean condition effect. **It is not one.**

| arm | 1 gw | 2 w3 | 3 gw | 4 w3 | 5 w3 | 6 gw | 7 w3 | 8 gw |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| stalled samples | 8 | 0 | 7 | 0 | **8** | **0** | 6 | 11 |
| host load, start -> end | 5.1 -> 19.4 | 18.3 -> 14.3 | 13.0 -> 10.2 | 12.2 -> 7.8 | 5.9 -> 18.8 | 18.3 -> 6.5 | 5.9 -> 7.9 | 6.6 -> 15.7 |

Arm 5 is an in-tab arm that stalled and arm 6 is a gateway arm that did not. **Four arms is not a
replicate**, and reading a condition effect off two versus two is the error the alternation exists to
prevent.

The three arms whose host load **ramped** (1, 5, 8, all starting near 5 and ending near 16 to 19)
carry the three worst counts, 8, 8 and 11. That is suggestive and it is not sufficient: arm 3 stalled
7 times on declining load and arm 7 stalled 6 times on flat load. **No mechanism is offered and none
is established.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ Why the main-thread result survives what the stall counts do not

Host load swung between **5.1 and 19.4 of 48 cores** across the sitting, driven by 40 neighbouring
bee nodes this project does not own. Across that 4x swing:

- the three in-tab arms read **0.360, 0.357, 0.355**
- the two gateway arms read **0.088, 0.089**

**The condition separates by 4x while the readings within a condition agree to 0.003.** A confound
that moved 4x and left no trace in the numbers is not driving them. ⚠️ That argument covers this
sitting's own comparison and **not** the 720p-versus-1080p one, which spans two nights at different
loads. Part of the extra load is the sitting's own cost: ffmpeg at 1080p takes 192% of a core against
roughly half that at 720p, and the browser 224%.

## What is deliberately not claimed

⛔⛔ **No latency figure appears above, and none should.** Three sittings at 1080p failed their
controls in 2026-08-07, two of them with control arms 0.92s apart against a 0.85s effect, and the
standing rule from that campaign is to stop measuring latency at this resolution. This sitting
measures processor cost and byte counts, both of which separated cleanly.

⛔ **Nothing here is a drift measurement.** Six-minute arms cannot resolve the +0.026/hr slope
`long-arm-drift-2026-08-14.md` measured over forty, and the within-arm slopes here are negative on
every arm, which is the settling of a fresh session and not a trend.

⚠️ **One sitting, one host, one broadcast.** The 720p comparison rests on two sittings run a day
apart.
