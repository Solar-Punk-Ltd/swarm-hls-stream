# ⛔ Do not go below a 0.5s GOP: one segment in five is not retrievable at the live edge

> ## ⛔⛔⛔ THE HEADLINE ABOVE IS WITHDRAWN, 2026-08-12
>
> A second sitting the same afternoon on the same rig gave **2.9%, 13.1% and 0.0%** where this one
> gave 18.3, 18.6 and 21.4. A rate that lands within 3 points one morning and spans the whole range
> that afternoon is not a property of the GOP.
>
> **Every refusal in that sitting was retrievable again within 140ms** (19 of 19, median 0.10s, none
> outstanding), and the mechanism is our own: `StreamUploader` writes the feed slot naming a segment
> with `deferred: false` and the segment bytes with `deferred: true`, so the reference is synced
> before the bytes are. A bench reader at the live edge asks too early and discards. A viewer's
> hls.js retries.
>
> ⛔ **"A viewer at the live edge cannot retrieve one segment in five" is false.**
> ✅ **0.5 remains the operating point**, on this sitting's stall and latency evidence and on #155
> making sub-0.5 segments unreachable from shipped config at all.
>
> See `gop-floor-replicate-2026-08-12.md`. Everything below is kept as the record of what this
> sitting measured.

**2026-08-12, funded, 0.8417 BZZ.** Six `bench:longrun` arms, GOP 0.25 against 0.50 at 720p30/2500k,
8 minutes each, round 0 discarded as warm-up and rounds 1 and 2 run in opposite order. Predictions
registered in `gop-floor-prediction-2026-08-12.md` before spending. All six arms exited clean.

**The sitting answers its question, but not with the number it went looking for.**

## The finding, and it is a retrieval failure rather than a latency result

| GOP | segment | reads that succeeded | reads that answered **404** | share |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **0.25** | 0.267s, ~110 KB | 76, 79, 66 | **17, 18, 18** | **18.3%, 18.6%, 21.4%** |
| 0.50 | 0.500s, ~200 KB | 368, 312, 349 | 0, 0, 0 | **0.0%** |

⭐⭐ **Zero at every other GOP.** Adding yesterday's sitting, GOP 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 give **0 discards
across 8 arms**, and GOP 0.25 gives 17 to 18 per arm across 3. Replicated 3 of 3 against 8 of 8, on
two sittings, on the same rig, with the arm order reversed between rounds.

Every discard is the same shape: the reference is published in the feed, the reader asks the gateway
for it, and `/bytes/<ref>` answers **404**.

⛔ **A viewer at the live edge cannot retrieve one segment in five at a 0.25s GOP.** Whether those
segments become retrievable later is **not measured here**, because the bench discards a refused
segment rather than waiting for it. That is the next question, not this one's answer.

## ⛔⛔ The latency comparison this sitting was designed to make is VOID

The medians looked decisive and must not be quoted:

| GOP | median capture to fetchable |
| ---: | ---: |
| 0.25 | 1015, 1081 ms |
| 0.50 | 1564, 1560 ms |

**Those are not comparable.** The 0.25 medians are computed after deleting 19% of the reads, and the
deleted ones are the failures. `longrun.ts` says so about itself, in the comment on the knob that was
left off:

> a refused segment is discarded, refused segments are by definition the slowest ones, and a median
> taken over what survives is flattered by exactly the samples that would have moved it

`BENCH_UNSERVED_BUDGET_MS` defaults to 0, and this sitting did not set it. **The instrument documented
the exact failure mode that invalidates the comparison, and the comment was not read until the result
looked surprising.** Same for the four hop predictions of `gop-floor-prediction`: `manifestPublish`
reading 186 ms against a predicted 225, and `fetch` reading 284 against a predicted 481, are hop
medians over the same censored sample. **None of the four is refuted, because none of them was
tested.**

## What survives, and what does not

✅ **Established.** The 404 rate at a 0.25s GOP is 18 to 21% against 0% everywhere else, replicated,
and it is a property of the arm rather than of one run.

✅ **Established.** Every arm sustained: zero confirmed feed stalls in all six, at both GOPs.

⛔ **Not established.** That a 0.25s GOP has lower latency. The measurement that says so is censored.

⛔ **Not established.** Any mechanism for the 404s. Two candidates are visible and **neither is
tested**: the segment may be too small to be retrievable as reliably (90 KB segments were separately
measured to fill the chunk window only 4.5%), or the reader may simply be outrunning propagation more
often when segments arrive 3.75 times a second. ⚠️ **No mechanism is claimed here.**

⚠️ **Unexplained and recorded rather than resolved.** The 0.25 arms read only **0.14 to 0.16 segments
per second** against 0.65 to 0.81 at every other GOP, which is roughly six times slower than their own
measured latency would predict. The 404s account for some of it and not obviously all of it. This is
an observation, not a finding.

## What it means for the product

⭐⭐⭐ **`DEFAULT_KNOBS.gopSeconds` should go to 0.5, and no lower.** The previous sitting established
that 0.5 beats 2.0 by 2.34s of latency and takes confirmed stalls from 3-of-3 to 0-of-3. This one
establishes that the next step down breaks retrieval at the live edge. **0.5 is a floor with evidence
on both sides of it rather than the lowest value anybody happened to sweep**, which is exactly what
this sitting was bought to determine.

The cost of that recommendation is unchanged: 19% more BZZ than a 2.0s GOP, or about **0.90 BZZ per
broadcast hour at 720p**.

## The next question, and it is narrow

**Do those 404s resolve, and how fast?** `BENCH_UNSERVED_WATCH_MS` times how long refused segments
stay refused **without waiting inside the collection loop**, so it answers this without perturbing the
loop's pace the way `BENCH_UNSERVED_BUDGET_MS` would. Three arms at a 0.25s GOP would cost about
**0.44 BZZ**.

⚠️ **It is not needed for the product decision**, which the table above already settles. It is worth
buying only if the intent is to rescue a sub-0.5 GOP rather than to ship one.
